    Conserved back to is the deepest branch of the tree of life where this protein still has a recognisable ortholog, from Universal (bacteria, archaea and eukaryotes alike) up to Mammalia. An arrow pointing down means the answer is older than your guess.

    Disease-linked is a yes/no: yes means UniProt lists at least one named inherited or acquired disease caused by variants in this protein, the kind with an entry in the catalogue of human genetic disorders. It is not "has been studied in a disease" — almost every protein has been. A quarter of the proteome qualifies; among answers, which are the well-studied ones, it is closer to half.

    Under each protein you guess is its family. That one is not scored and not in the shared grid; it turns green when it is the answer's family and amber when the two are related, which is the quickest way to tell a near miss from a wrong turn.

    Amber means different things per column: one rung away on the conservation ladder, within 10% on length, an overlapping localization or pathway, or a different function in the same family — guessing a kinase when the answer is a protease gets you amber, because both are enzymes.
